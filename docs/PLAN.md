# Baker Family Reunion 2027 — Website Plan

## Goal
A simple, mobile-friendly site that gives every branch of the family one
place for dates, travel, lodging, the schedule and RSVPs.

## Key facts
- **Website:** reunion.twentyroosroad.com (to be set up)
- **Location:** Sandwich, MA (Cape Cod), with a day trip to Boston
- **Dates:** July 4th week 2027. Proposed core window **Wed Jun 30 – Wed Jul 7**
  (see [travel-research.md](travel-research.md))
- **Coming from:** Bend OR, Portland OR, Santa Barbara CA, San Diego CA,
  Los Angeles CA, Atlanta GA, Columbus OH (flying), plus New Jersey,
  Altamont NY (upstate) and Long Beach NY (driving)
- **History:** this is the **second** Baker Family Reunion. The first was in
  2022.

## Design & tone: the whole site (not built yet)

**The big idea:** a family reunion website that takes itself *very*
seriously. It should look as polished as a real company's site, with
clean layout, confident typography and crisp photos, while the copy keeps
quietly winking. **Professional on the surface, family reunion underneath.**
The humor is in deadpan details, never slapstick.

### Visual direction
- **Palette: "Cape Cod, but make it corporate"**
  - Deep **navy** (headers, footer)
  - **Sand / off-white** (page background)
  - **Sea-glass green** and **sky blue** (accents, links). The blues pair
    nicely with the Agent Relay logo
  - **Cranberry red** for highlights and calls to action, a nod to
    Alexander Boght, cranberry bog foreman (see Our Story on Home)
- **Type:** an elegant serif for headlines (e.g. Fraunces or Playfair) and
  a clean sans-serif for body text (e.g. Inter). It should read like an
  annual report.
- **Imagery: caricature cartoons.** People, families and scenes are drawn
  as friendly **caricatures**: big expressive heads, small bodies,
  exaggerated signature features and props, bold outlines, and the soft
  watercolor coloring of the house painting. Icons (croquet mallet, bocce
  ball, lobster, golf cart, dice) are drawn in the same cartoon style. Real
  photos appear only in the Photos tab and the house carousel. See
  "Illustration style: caricature cartoons" below.
- **Why it works:** the polished, annual-report layout plus goofy
  caricatures is the joke. The design says "Fortune 500," and the drawings
  say "family reunion."
- **Details:** thin nautical-stripe dividers, rounded cards, generous white
  space, light motion only (a gentle hover lift, nothing bouncy).
- **Mobile-first** (see the next section), accessible contrast, and it works
  in light mode, with dark mode optional.

### Illustration style: caricature cartoons

**The house stays the core illustration.** Keep the existing watercolor of
the house as the centerpiece and anchor of the style, and create a
**caricature version of it** in the same palette to use as the main hero.
It should still be instantly recognizable, but cartoon-ified:
- Slightly squashed and stretched proportions, a gently leaning chimney,
  windows a touch too big, an extra-plump roofline
- Bold ink outline over the watercolor wash, so it matches the people
- Optional tiny gags: a little ⚡ sign by the door reading "Electricity:
  est. 1963," and a croquet mallet leaning on the steps
- The original watercolor is still used where something calmer fits: the
  Our Story section, the app icon and the "Where to stay" card

**People caricatures: the rules**
- **Exaggerate the fun, never the flaws.** Bigger smiles, bigger hair,
  bigger personality, and each person's signature thing (Amanda's swing,
  Uncle Sean's whistle, Uncle Mike's chef hat) gets supersized. No jokes
  about weight, age, hairlines or anything people are sensitive about.
- **Big head, small body** (about 1:2 head-to-body ratio), expressive
  eyebrows, simple dot-or-oval eyes, rosy cheeks.
- **Bold, slightly wobbly ink outlines** with **soft watercolor fills** in
  the site palette (navy, sand, sea-glass, sky blue, cranberry), so the
  cartoons and the house look like they came from the same sketchbook.
- **Props tell the story:** golf cart, mac and cheese pot, whistle and
  clipboard, softball bat, board games, lobster bib.
- **Kids are drawn extra-cute,** never mocking, using first names only.
- **Everyone approves their own caricature** before it goes on the site
  (parents approve for kids). Offer a "redraw me" option, no questions asked.

**Consistency checklist** (so it looks like one artist drew everything)
- Same outline weight, palette, head-to-body ratio and paper texture on
  every image
- Same light direction (soft, from the upper left, like the house painting)
- Transparent backgrounds for people and props, so they layer anywhere
- Draw each person once as a **"character sheet"** (front view plus 2–3
  expressions) and reuse that look across every scene

### Humor guidelines (so it stays subtle)
- **About one joke per section.** Let the design do the "professional" part.
- **Deadpan corporate voice** applied to family things. That's the
  running gag.
- **Never at a specific relative's expense** (no names in jokes unless
  they've opted in), and nothing that excludes in-laws or newcomers.
- Jokes should never get in the way of real info: dates, addresses and
  sign-ups stay crystal clear.

### Running gag: The Trophy 🏆

The family has a **gold crown trophy** awarded at the reunion competition.
Most people don't know what the competition actually *is*, nobody can
explain how you win, and the tallying system makes no sense. The site
treats it with **total, unwavering seriousness**, which is the joke.
*(Confirm the competition's official name and past winners. The trophy
has names engraved on it.)*

**Where it shows up**
- **Home:** a small gold trophy icon in the header. Tapping it opens **"The
  Trophy"** page/sheet.
- **The Trophy page** (linked from Home and the Schedule tab):
  - A caricature of the trophy, glowing, on a velvet pedestal
  - **Reigning champion(s)** with a caricature portrait. If it's the
    Altamont Bakers, use Jenny's proud eyes-closed grin.
  - **Hall of Champions:** past winners by year, copied from the
    engravings
  - **Official Rules:** a document that is almost entirely
    ~~redacted~~ black bars, with one readable line: *"Points shall be
    awarded."*
  - **FAQ:**
    - *"What is the competition?"* Yes.
    - *"How do I win?"* By having more points.
    - *"How do I get points?"* That's not how points work.
    - *"Who keeps score?"* The Committee. *(There is no Committee.)*
  - **Live Standings board** during reunion week: a leaderboard with
    confidently precise but unexplained numbers (e.g. *Altamont Bakers:
    14¾ · Bend Bakers: −2 · Thatcher: 31 (under review)*). Point
    changes come with cryptic notes like *"+3, see Rule 7(b)"* (there is
    no Rule 7(b)) or *"−½, croquet incident, details sealed."*
  - **Tally method:** a rotating explanation that changes every page
    load: *"Tallies are calculated using the Modified Baker Method,"*
    *"…the Gramps Coefficient,"* *"…a system that predates
    electricity at the house (1963)."*
- **Schedule tab:** a "🏆 Trophy Ceremony" event at the farewell dinner
  (Tue Jul 6), with the description: *"Results final. Results may change."*
- **Everyday events quietly award points:** small "+1 🏆" chips appear on
  random Schedule items (hosting dinner, winning bocce, arriving on time),
  with no explanation. Nothing explains which ones count.
- **Data:** the organizer can update standings in a **"Trophy" tab of the
  Google Sheet** (Team · Points · Note), so the leaderboard updates live.
  Note column for the cryptic explanations.

**Keep it kind:** the joke is the mystery and the over-seriousness,
never anyone's score. No last place shaming. Negative points always come
with an absurd, obviously fake reason.

## Mobile-first web app (applies to every page)

Most of the family will use this on their phones: at the airport, in the
car and on the lawn. So **design for a phone first**, then let it stretch
to tablets and desktops. It should feel like an app, not a website.

### Install it like an app (PWA)
- Build it as a **Progressive Web App**: "Add to Home Screen" on iPhone and
  Android puts a **Baker Reunion app icon** (the watercolor house) on the
  phone, which opens full-screen without the browser bars.
- A friendly prompt on the Home page: *"Install the app. It's free, and
  frankly the best app you'll download this year."* With a short "how
  to" for iPhone (Share → Add to Home Screen).
- **Works offline / on bad Cape cell service:** the essentials are saved
  on the phone: house address and directions, the itinerary, the calendar,
  sign-ups as last loaded, Explore and the FAQ. Shows "Last updated
  2 hours ago" when offline.
- Optional, later: **push notifications** for reunion-week announcements
  ("Dinner is at 6," "Golf carts have arrived"). Works on Android, and on
  iPhone once the app is installed to the home screen.

### Navigation
- **Bottom tab bar** (thumb-friendly), always visible on phones, with the
  5 tabs (see "Site structure"):
  🏠 **Home** · 📅 **Schedule** · ✈️ **Trip** · 🏖️ **Explore** · 📸 **Photos**
- No "More" menu: everything fits. Our Story and the FAQ are linked from
  Home and the footer.
- On desktop, the same 5 items become a normal top menu.
- **During reunion week**, the Home tab becomes a "**Today**" view: today's
  events, tonight's dinner and game-night hosts, the weather, and who's
  arriving or leaving.

### Phone-friendly design rules
- **Big tap targets** (at least 44×44 px), with buttons within thumb reach
  near the bottom. Main actions (Sign up, Upload, RSVP) are **sticky at
  the bottom** of their pages.
- **One column** on phones, with cards instead of tables. Every table in
  this plan (city tips, beaches, restaurants) becomes a stack of
  swipeable or tappable cards.
- **Calendar:** on phones it defaults to a **day-by-day agenda list** with
  the colored chips, and the month grid is optional.
- **Maps:** the "Where we're coming from" map simplifies on small screens
  (pins + list). Every address is a **tap to open in Apple/Google Maps**
  link.
- **Tap-to-act everywhere:** tap to call (restaurants, C2 Vehicles), tap
  to text the organizer, tap for directions.
- **Forms built for thumbs:** native date and time pickers, the right
  keyboards (numbers for flight numbers, etc.), autofill, short forms, and
  big "Done" buttons. The photo upload opens the **camera roll or camera**
  directly.
- **Bottom sheets** instead of pop-ups for details (event details, a
  restaurant card), swipe down to close.
- **Hero illustration:** the caricature house fits the phone screen, and
  vignettes become a swipeable character-card strip (see Home page spec).

### Speed (Cape Cod cell service is not great)
- Lightweight pages: aim for the Home page to load in **under 2 seconds on
  a slow 4G connection**.
- Images in modern formats (WebP/AVIF), sized for phones, lazy-loaded.
- Minimal JavaScript. The sign-up, travel and photo data load after the
  page shows, with skeleton placeholders ("Herding cousins…").

### Testing
- Test on a real **iPhone (Safari)** and a real **Android (Chrome)** before
  sharing, plus a small phone (iPhone SE size) and a tablet.
- Check installing to the home screen, offline mode, photo upload from the
  camera roll, and sign-ups on cellular.
- Accessibility: readable text sizes (16px+), works with larger system
  fonts, VoiceOver/TalkBack labels, and reduced-motion support.

## Site structure: 5 tabs

Consolidated from 11 pages so everything fits in a phone's bottom tab bar
with **no "More" menu**. Each tab answers one question.

| Tab | Answers | Combines |
|---|---|---|
| 🏠 **Home** | *What's going on?* | Home + About ("Our Story") + Family ("Meet the Bakers"). Becomes **"Today"** during reunion week |
| 📅 **Schedule** | *When is everything?* | Calendar + Itinerary + dinner/game-night sign-ups |
| ✈️ **Trip** | *How do I get there and where do I stay?* | RSVP + Travel + Accommodation + golf carts. RSVP and travel plans are **one form** |
| 🏖️ **Explore** | *What should we do?* | Things to Do ("Tubby's Guide"): restaurants, beaches, activities |
| 📸 **Photos** | *Show me the pictures* | Upload, live gallery, downloads, Photo Challenge |

- **FAQ** is no longer a tab. Each answer lives on the page it's about
  (airports on Trip, Google accounts on Photos, etc.), plus a short
  general FAQ linked from the footer (what to pack, who to contact).
- **Footer links:** Our Story · FAQ · Contact the Reunion Committee ·
  Sponsor.
- Tab names are easy to change later (e.g. "Trip" could be "Getting Here").

**Making the illustrations (AI-generated)**
- Generate with an AI image tool, using family photos as **likeness
  reference** and the house watercolor as the **color and texture
  reference**, so everything looks like one artist made it.
- Order of work:
  1. **Caricature house** first (it sets the style for everything else)
  2. A **character sheet** for each person (approve the likeness once)
  3. Then the **scenes** and **family portraits**, reusing those characters
- Shared style prompt (starting point):
  > *Friendly caricature cartoon, big expressive head on a small body,
  > exaggerated smile and signature features, bold slightly wobbly ink
  > outlines with soft watercolor fills on textured paper, palette of navy,
  > sand, sea-glass green, sky blue and cranberry red, warm and
  > affectionate, never unflattering, transparent background, no text.*
- Caricature house prompt (starting point):
  > *[style prompt] + a caricature of this exact Cape Cod saltbox house
  > (reference image), playfully exaggerated proportions, slightly leaning
  > chimney, oversized windows, plump roofline, same cream siding and
  > brown roof, lush cartoon shrubs, still clearly the same house.*
- Scene prompts, one per vignette, e.g.:
  > *[style prompt] + a caricature of the man in the reference photo, giant
  > grin, driving a comically small six-seat golf cart with a canopy down a gravel driveway
  > toward a white picket gate, small sign on the cart reads "#1".*
- Output: **transparent PNGs** (so they layer over the house), about 2×
  display size for sharp screens.
- Generate a few options per scene, and let the family vote on favorites
  (it could even be an RSVP-page poll).
- **Get a yes from each person** (or their parents for kids) before their

> bedrooms and two full bathrooms. Alexander would be astonished, mostly
> by the bathrooms. In 2021 it became ours, and now it's yours for a week.

  - "Download all" link: opens the Google Photos album (shared by link),

## Tech approach (proposal)
- **Mobile-first Progressive Web App**: a static site (plain HTML/CSS, or a
  lightweight generator like Astro or Eleventy) in this repo, plus a **web
  app manifest** (name, icons, theme colors) and a **service worker** for
  offline caching
- Hosted free on **GitHub Pages** (HTTPS, which PWAs require)
- **Web address: `reunion.twentyroosroad.com`**, a subdomain of
  twentyroosroad.com, like the wedding website.
  - **Owner:** talk to **Will Washburn's agent** about setting it up, since
    they manage the twentyroosroad.com domain/DNS.
  - **What's needed:**
    1. A DNS **CNAME record**: `reunion` → `marybees.github.io`
    2. A `CNAME` file in this repo containing `reunion.twentyroosroad.com`
       (or set it under the repo's Settings → Pages → Custom domain)
    3. Turn on **Enforce HTTPS** in Pages settings once the certificate is
       issued (usually within an hour)
    4. Optional: verify the domain in GitHub account settings to protect
       it from takeover
  - Use the custom address everywhere people see it: QR codes, the
    save-the-date, the installed app, and the Apps Script settings that
    allow the site to send sign-ups and photos.
  - The address includes the house's street name, which is fine for
    family. It's another reason to keep the full address and personal
    details off the public pages.
- **RSVP is part of the Trip tab's "RSVP & travel plans" form** and is saved to
  the same Google Sheet (the RSVP & Travel Plans tab below). No separate
  Google Form.
- **Sign-ups are stored in the family Google Sheet** (decided):
  https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1-NZnvlRHNE7zUl2nkmGNHLoTnw7PlUIWrsRG9bzwx3Y/edit

  | Sheet tab | Columns |
  |---|---|
  | **Game Night Host Signup** | Date · Game · Hosted by |
  | **Dinner Host Signup** | Date · Cuisine · Provided by |
  | **Trophy** *(new tab to add)* | Team · Points · Note (for the cryptic explanations) |
  | **RSVP & Travel Plans** *(new tab to add)* | Family · Coming? (Yes/Maybe/No) · Adults · Kids · Dietary Notes · Golf Cart Interest · From · Mode (Fly/Drive) · Arrival Date · Arrival Time · Arrival Airport · Arrival Flight · Departure Date · Departure Time · Departure Airport · Departure Flight · Staying At · Excited For · Needs Ride · Seats to Share |

  - **Two-way sync:** the website both **reads from** and **writes to** the
    sheet. Signing up on the site adds a row to the sheet, and typing in the
    sheet shows up on the site.
  - **Writing:** a small **Google Apps Script** attached to the sheet,
    deployed as a web app. The site's sign-up form sends Date + Game + Hosted
    by (or Date + Cuisine + Provided by), and the script adds a row to the
    right tab. It rejects a second sign-up for a date that's already taken.
  - **Reading:** the same script returns both tabs as JSON, and the
    Schedule tab's sign-up board and calendar build themselves from it. One source of
    truth.
  - **Editing:** the organizer can fix or remove sign-ups directly in the
    sheet, and the site picks up the change on the next page load.
  - **Date format:** use one consistent format in the Date column (e.g.
    `2027-06-30`), or format the column as a date, so the site matches rows
    to calendar days reliably.
  - **Security:** never put the sheet's edit link on the public site. The
    sheet's own sharing can stay private, since the script runs as the
    sheet owner.
  - Upgrade path: a hosted database (e.g. Firebase or Supabase) only if we
    later want logins, instant updates or tournament scoring.
- **Photos app: how it works** (Google Apps Script + Google Photos Library
  API, under the organizer's Google account)
  - **One-time setup:**
    1. Create a Google Cloud project, enable the **Photos Library API**, and
       link it to the Apps Script project.
    2. Scopes: `photoslibrary.appendonly` (upload and create albums) and
       `photoslibrary.readonly.appcreateddata` (read back what the app
       uploaded). Add `photoslibrary.edit.appcreateddata` if the site
       should be able to remove photos.
    3. The script creates the album **"Baker Family Reunion 2027"** once
       and saves its ID.
    4. The organizer opens Google Photos and **shares that album by link**
       by hand, since the API can no longer share albums. *(Confirm the
       Google Photos app allows sharing an app-created album. Expected,
       but verify during setup.)*
  - **Upload flow:** the browser shrinks each photo (e.g. 2560px JPEG)
    → sends it to the script → the script uploads the bytes to Google
    Photos and adds the item to the album, with "Added by [name]" as the
    description.
  - **Gallery flow:** the script lists the album's items and returns short-
    lived image links. The site shows thumbnails (`=w400-h400`) and full-size
    downloads (`=d`). Links expire after about an hour, so the page fetches
    fresh ones on each visit.
  - **Limits to plan around:** photos yes, but long videos are risky (Apps
    Script request size limits, about 50 MB). The API's daily quota is far
    more than a family reunion needs. Uploads count against the organizer's
    Google storage.
  - **Gotcha to check:** if the Google Cloud project's sign-in screen stays
    in "Testing" mode, Google may expire the app's authorization every 7
    days. Set it to "In production" (fine for personal use, with an
    "unverified app" warning only the organizer sees), or re-authorize
    weekly during reunion week.
  - Same passcode check as the sign-ups, enforced in the script, not just
    the page.
- Keep it light on privacy: first names or family names only, no phone
  numbers or emails shown publicly.
